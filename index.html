Stephen Mrenna

Senior Scientist · Scientific Computing and AI Directorate · Fermilab

Stephen Mrenna is a particle physicist at the Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory (Fermilab). He is one of the primary authors of the Pythia event generator, a tool used extensively by theorists and experimentalists to interpret collider data, and a member of the CMS collaboration at the Large Hadron Collider.

Research

Pythia & Event Generation

Co-author of Pythia, the Monte Carlo event generator used across the field to simulate the full chain of a particle collision — from the hard process through parton showers, hadronization, and decay. Pythia underpins simulation for essentially every LHC experiment and many other collider and cosmic-ray programs.

Machine Learning for Particle Physics

Developing machine-learning methods to improve and fit event-generator models directly to data — normalizing-flow models of hadronization, simulation-based inference techniques, and differentiable, autodifferentiation-driven fitting procedures.

Quantum Computing for Particle Physics

Exploring simple models of confining theories to learn more about hadronization models.
